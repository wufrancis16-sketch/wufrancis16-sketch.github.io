Infor CloudSuite vs Epicor Kinetic: Industry ERP Comparison

Two industry-focused ERPs compared for manufacturing, distribution, and vertical depth, with guidance on which wins by sector and deployment style.

  • Infor CloudSuite: industry templates
  • Epicor Kinetic: configurable manufacturing

  • Buyer pain points and trade-offs

Infor CloudSuite and Epicor Kinetic are built for industry rather than generic finance. Both target manufacturers and distributors, but they differentiate by vertical depth and by how much you configure versus how much comes pre-packaged. If your selection criterion is 'does it understand my shop floor,' this is the comparison that matters, because horizontal ERPs often force you to rebuild industry logic that these two deliver natively.

Infor CloudSuite: industry templates

Infor organizes CloudSuite into industry-specific editions such as CloudSuite Industrial, CloudSuite Fashion, and CloudSuite Food & Beverage, each with pre-built process models and a modern user experience on the Infor OS platform running on AWS. It is strong in fashion, food and beverage, healthcare, and aerospace, where regulatory and lot-traceability requirements are demanding. The appeal is a head start: you adopt an industry model instead of building one from a blank canvas, which shortens time-to-value for standard processes.

Epicor Kinetic: configurable manufacturing

Epicor Kinetic focuses on discrete and process manufacturing, make-to-order and configure-to-order environments, and distribution. It is highly configurable and popular with mid-market manufacturers that run complex job shops where every order differs. Rather than a fixed vertical template, Epicor gives you a flexible toolkit to model your exact routing, scheduling, and costing, which suits businesses with proprietary processes that off-the-shelf models cannot represent without heavy compromise.

Feature emphasis

Both cover MRP, CRP, quality, and shop-floor execution. Infor's differentiator is industry content and a consumer-grade UX; Epicor's is operational configurability and strong estimating-to-cash for custom manufacturing. CloudSuite's analytics and multi-tenant cloud operations are polished, while Epicor's strength is fitting unusual manufacturing workflows without heavy custom code, keeping upgrades safer over time.

  • Infor CloudSuite: packaged vertical models, modern UX, strong in fashion, food, healthcare, aerospace.
  • Epicor Kinetic: deep job-shop and MTO configurability, strong distribution and process manufacturing.
  • Both: require experienced industry implementation partners, not generalists.

Pricing and deployment

Typical market ranges, confirm with vendor, place both in the mid-market to upper mid-market band with per-user subscription and implementation services billed separately; CloudSuite's cloud operations are included in its multi-tenant model, while Epicor offers cloud, on-premise, and hybrid. Total cost is driven less by license than by the number of sites, modules, and the partner's industry expertise, so the implementation quote matters more than the list price.

Adoption and change management

Industry ERPs succeed when shop-floor users actually adopt the system instead of reverting to paper and spreadsheets. Infor's polished UX helps adoption in offices; Epicor's configurability helps engineers model real work. In both cases, training supervisors and capturing feedback during a phased rollout predicts success better than the sophistication of the software features themselves.

Buyer pain points and trade-offs

CloudSuite buyers must accept the vendor's industry model, which can feel constraining if your process is highly unique. Epicor buyers gain flexibility but carry more responsibility for designing the system correctly. Both depend on a knowledgeable implementation partner; a weak partner turns either into an expensive disappointment regardless of product strength.

  • Infor trade-off: fast industry start versus conformity to the template.
  • Epicor trade-off: fit-for-purpose flexibility versus heavier design responsibility.
  • Both: partner industry experience is the single biggest success factor.

Decision framework

Match the product to your sector and to how standardized your processes are before requesting proposals.

  • Choose Infor CloudSuite if you operate in a packaged vertical (fashion, food, healthcare, aerospace) and want industry templates live fast.
  • Choose Epicor Kinetic if you run custom, make-to-order, or complex job-shop manufacturing needing deep configuration.
  • Choose hybrid-capable Epicor if data residency or on-premise integration is required.
  • Choose Infor if a modern multi-tenant cloud UX is a priority for user adoption.

When to look beyond both

Neither product covers every manufacturer. If you are a global enterprise with complex multi-plant processes, SAP S/4HANA may be the more natural fit than either industry specialist. If you are a small job shop with a thin budget, a lighter cloud tool or even strong accounting plus inventory software may be enough. Choose the product whose industry model matches your sub-vertical closely, because forcing a mismatched template costs more than the license savings.

  • Skip both if you are a tiny shop needing only basic inventory and invoicing.
  • Skip Infor if your process is too unique for any packaged vertical template.
  • Skip Epicor if you lack the engineering capacity to configure it properly.

A useful exercise is to list your five most painful shop-floor exceptions and ask each vendor to demonstrate them live; the one that handles exceptions natively, rather than via custom code, will cost less to maintain. Exception handling is where industry ERPs truly differentiate, and it is the dimension demos most often gloss over.

Shortlist by running a reference call with a customer in your exact sub-vertical, then scope a pilot of your hardest routing or recipe. The platform whose model your engineers argue with least is the right one, and that evidence matters more than analyst rankings.
