Odoo Pricing 2026: Community vs Enterprise — Is Free Really Free?

Odoo's open-source Community edition vs the paid Enterprise cloud. A candid look at what 'free' costs you in practice.

Odoo is the most popular open-source ERP in the world, and its free Community edition is genuinely capable. But the word free in ERP is always qualified, and Odoo is no exception. Understanding the line between Community and Enterprise is the difference between a smart saving and a false economy that costs more in developer time than a subscription would have.

Community edition: free, with strings

Community is free to download and self-host, with no license fee and no user limit. You get the core applications: sales, inventory, purchasing, manufacturing, CRM, project, website, e-commerce, and basic accounting through invoicing. The trade-offs are real. There is no official mobile app, no Odoo Studio no-code customizer, and no certified local accounting reports in many regions. You are responsible for hosting, security patching, backups, version upgrades, and support. In practice the real cost is your system administrator's time or a hosting partner, typically $10 to $50 per month for a small instance and more as you scale and add worker capacity.

The One App Free model

Odoo also offers a cloud option where a single application is free for unlimited users. The moment you add a second app, you move to the full Enterprise per-user rate. This is a common and affordable way for a small team to start with, say, CRM alone before committing to the full suite, and it lets a business validate Odoo without any license risk before expanding into finance and inventory.

Enterprise edition: what you pay

Enterprise is a per-user subscription that unlocks every Odoo app plus Studio, the mobile app, hosting, automatic upgrades, and official support. In 2026 the Standard plan runs about $24.90 per user per month billed annually and includes all apps on Odoo Online. The Custom plan runs about $37.40 per user per month and adds Odoo Studio, multi-company consolidation, external API access, and the choice of Odoo.sh or self-hosting. External portal users such as customers are free; you pay only for employees who create and edit records, which keeps the effective per-seat cost lower than the raw rate implies.

  • Community: $0 license, self-hosted, no Studio, no mobile, no official support.
  • Enterprise Standard: about $24.90 per user per month, all apps, Odoo Online hosting.
  • Enterprise Custom: about $37.40 per user per month, adds Studio, multi-company, API, Odoo.sh.
  • Implementation via Odoo partners: typically $5,000 to $50,000 depending on scope.

Hosting is a separate layer

On Enterprise, hosting is included on Odoo Online or Odoo.sh. On Community, you pay for a server yourself, commonly $60 to $500 per month depending on workers and staging environments. A solo founder can run all-in around $85 per month; a 10-user SMB lands near $2,800 per month in year one once implementation is amortized, then closer to $370 per month ongoing. The license number alone is misleading either way, which is why the three-layer model of license, hosting, and implementation must be budgeted together rather than separately.

Hidden costs and pain points

The classic Odoo trap is underestimating the three layers: license, hosting, and implementation. Teams that self-host Community to avoid the fee often spend more on a developer than the Enterprise subscription would have cost. Another pain point is local compliance: in some markets the Community accounting module lacks the tax-filing integration that Enterprise provides, which can force paid bridging software and ongoing manual work. Module creep is milder than on per-module platforms because Enterprise bundles all apps, but implementation scope still grows as business needs surface during the build.

Objective value assessment

Migrating from Community to Enterprise

Many businesses start on Community and move to Enterprise once they need mobility or compliance. The migration is well documented and usually straightforward because both editions share the same PostgreSQL codebase, so your data carries over without a rebuild. The cost of switching is the subscription plus any implementation cleanup, not a re-platforming project. Plan the move before you write heavy custom code on Community, because deeply customized Community deployments take more effort to upgrade than a lightly configured one. If you know mobility or certified local tax reporting will be required within a year, starting on Enterprise avoids a second project.

The common underestimation

The most frequent Odoo budgeting error is quoting only the per-user license and forgetting hosting and implementation. A business that self-hosts to dodge the fee often spends more on a developer than the Enterprise subscription would have cost, and still lacks vendor support. Treat the three layers as one budget from day one, and the true all-in monthly number will not surprise you at renewal.

Community is a strong choice for technically comfortable small teams, early startups validating a model, or companies with a developer on staff who can use the OCA community module ecosystem to close feature gaps at no license cost. Enterprise is fairly priced for companies that want mobility, Studio customization, and vendor support without running a server. Watch the per-app versus full-suite jump and decide early whether you need Custom for Studio or multi-company. The per-user rates above are Odoo's published 2026 figures; implementation and hosting costs vary by partner and should be confirmed directly before you commit.
